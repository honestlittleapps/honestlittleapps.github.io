Fight Better: a Free 5-Day Course for Couples

Every couple argues. What matters is how. Five short lessons you can read together or alone, each with one thing to try this week. 5 lessons · about 10 minutes each · free, no sign-up.

Get a reminder each day: Works with Apple, Google and Outlook calendars. Starts tomorrow.

What you’ll do

  1. Start gently
  2. Take a real break when you’re flooded
  3. Use repair attempts
  4. Listen until they feel heard
  5. Make a weekly check-in
Day 1

Start gently

How a hard conversation starts often decides how it ends. Relationship researcher John Gottman found that harsh start-ups usually lead to harsh endings.

  1. Swap “You never…” for “I feel… about… and I need…”.
  2. Complain about one situation, not about your partner’s character.
  3. Pick a time when neither of you is hungry, rushed or half-asleep.

Today: Rewrite one recent complaint as a gentle start-up.

Day 2

Take a real break when you’re flooded

When your heart is racing, you can’t think clearly or listen well. Gottman’s research suggests a break of at least 20 minutes to calm down.

  1. Agree on a signal or phrase for “I need a break.”
  2. Say when you will come back: “Give me 30 minutes, then let’s finish this.”
  3. During the break, do something calming. Don’t rehearse your argument.

Today: Agree on your break signal together today.

Day 3

Use repair attempts

A repair attempt is anything that stops a fight from escalating: a joke, a touch, “I’m sorry, can we start over?”

  1. Notice the repairs your partner already makes, and accept them.
  2. Have two phrases ready, such as “That came out wrong” or “I see your point.”
  3. Repair the next day too: “I didn’t like how I spoke last night.”

Today: Tell your partner one repair attempt that works on you.

Day 4

Listen until they feel heard

People can’t move on until they feel understood. Understanding is not the same as agreeing.

  1. Take turns: one speaks and the other only listens and asks questions.
  2. Repeat back what you heard: “So you felt… when…”.
  3. Ask “Did I get that right?” before you give your side.

Today: Try one 10-minute conversation using these turns.

Day 5

Make a weekly check-in

Small issues talked about early rarely become big fights.

  1. Choose a weekly time: 20 minutes, phones away.
  2. Use three questions: What went well this week? What was hard? What do you need next week?
  3. End with one thing you appreciated about each other.

Today: Put the first check-in in both your calendars.

Questions

Is this couples therapy?

No. It is general relationship education. If there is abuse, fear or ongoing harm, please contact a professional or a local support service.

Do we both need to do it?

It works best together, but one person changing how they start and repair conversations can change the pattern.
