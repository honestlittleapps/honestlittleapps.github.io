Questions to Ask Your Teenager

Teens talk when it feels like a conversation, not a check-up. These questions ask for their opinion, which most teens are happy to give.

Easy openers

  1. What's something you've been listening to a lot lately?
  2. What's the most overrated thing right now?
  3. What's a show or video you think I'd actually like?
  4. What's the best thing you ate this week?
  5. If you had a free Saturday with no rules, what would you do?
  6. What's a trend you don't get?
  7. What's something you're good at that I might not know about?
  8. Who's the funniest person in your year?
  9. What's one app you'd delete forever if you could?
  10. What's the best advice you've heard recently?

Their opinions

  1. What do adults get wrong about your generation?
  2. What rule of ours do you think is unfair, and why?
  3. If you ran the school, what's the first thing you'd change?
  4. What makes a teacher good?
  5. What do you think about how much time people spend on their phones?
  6. What's a cause you care about?
  7. Who's someone online you think is actually worth following?
  8. What's something you've changed your mind about?
  9. Is it harder or easier being a teenager now than when I was one?
  10. What's something you wish you'd learned earlier?

Future

  1. If money didn't matter, what job would you try?
  2. Where would you like to live someday?
  3. What's something you want to learn this year?
  4. What's one thing you'd want your own kids to have that you had?
  5. What do you want to be known for?
  6. What's a skill you think everyone should learn?
  7. What would a perfect day look like when you're 25?
  8. Is there anything you'd like to try that we could do together?
  9. What's a goal you haven't told anyone about?
  10. What's scary about growing up, and what's exciting?

Friends and life

  1. Who do you feel most yourself around?
  2. What makes someone a good friend?
  3. Is there anyone you're worried about?
  4. What's the best thing about your friend group right now?
  5. Has anyone surprised you lately?
  6. What's stressing you out at the moment?
  7. What helps you relax?
  8. When do you feel most confident?
  9. What's something you'd like more of from me?
  10. What's something you'd like less of from me?

Deeper, when the moment's right

  1. What's something you're proud of that nobody noticed?
  2. What's the hardest thing about being you right now?
  3. If you could tell your younger self one thing, what would it be?
  4. What do you think matters most in life?
  5. Is there anything you wish we talked about more?
  6. What do you do when you feel down?
  7. What do you believe about God, and has it changed?
  8. What's a memory from when you were little that you love?
  9. What does a good family look like to you?
  10. Is there anything you need from me that you haven't asked for?

Questions

How do I get my teenager to talk to me?

Ask for their opinion rather than a report, talk side by side (in the car, cooking, walking), and listen more than you advise.

What if they give one-word answers?

Try again another day without pushing. Teens often open up later in the evening or when you least expect it.
