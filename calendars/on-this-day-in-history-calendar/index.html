Anniversaries are a great way into history, but "on this day" lists often stop at the headline. Each event here adds a detective question: who wrote the source, when, and what might they have left out or tidied up?

There are 37 well-documented dates across 2,000 years, including Caesar, Hastings, Magna Carta, the Titanic, D-Day and Apollo 11. Teachers can use them as five-minute starters, and they're great at the dinner table.

How to add it on iPhone

  1. Open this page in Safari on your iPhone.
  2. Tap Add to iPhone Calendar, then tap Subscribe.
  3. Tap Add. Events appear in the Calendar app within a minute.

On a Mac, the same button opens Calendar. In Google Calendar, use the Google button, or paste https://honestlittleapps.github.io/calendars/on-this-day-in-history-calendar/on-this-day-history.ics into Other calendars › From URL.

Questions

Are the dates reliable?

They're widely documented. Where historians disagree, like Pompeii's eruption date or Luther's 95 Theses, the event says so.

Which calendar are old dates in?

Dates before 1582 are given as traditionally reported (Julian calendar), for example the Ides of March in 44 BC.

What's PastRush?

A history game where you order events and weigh sources from case files, like a detective.

Is this calendar free?

Yes. It costs nothing, needs no account and shows no ads. You can unsubscribe at any time.

How do I remove it from my iPhone?

Open the Calendar app, tap Calendars at the bottom, tap the ⓘ next to this calendar and choose Delete Calendar. Or go to Settings › Apps › Calendar › Calendar Accounts › Subscribed Calendars.
