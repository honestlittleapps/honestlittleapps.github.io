2-minute symptom log · 8:00 PM

Quick log for the week: sleep, hot flushes, mood, energy, cycle. Patterns are much easier to spot (and to explain at an appointment) when they're written down weekly instead of remembered.

Monthly menopause check-in

Five minutes, once a month: • How many hot flushes or night sweats this month, roughly? • Sleep: better, same or worse? • Periods: dates, flow, any gaps or unusual bleeding? • Mood, brain fog, joint aches, anything new?

Jan4Mon
Before your appointment: prepare your summary

New year, new appointment? Bring a one-page summary: symptoms with rough frequency, cycle dates, sleep, medications and supplements, and your top 3 questions. It makes a 10-minute appointment far more useful.

Oct18Mon
World Menopause Day

October 18 is World Menopause Day, promoted by the International Menopause Society. A good day to book the check-up you've been putting off, or to share what you've learned with a friend.

Perimenopause can last several years, and symptoms come and go. That makes them hard to describe from memory. This calendar asks the same few questions every Sunday evening and on the 1st of each month, so patterns show up: sleep getting worse, flushes clustering, cycles stretching out.

It also marks World Menopause Day (October 18) and a January reminder to prepare a one-page summary before your next appointment. The calendar doesn't store anything you write. That stays wherever you choose to log it.

How to add it on iPhone

  1. Open this page in Safari on your iPhone.
  2. Tap Add to iPhone Calendar, then tap Subscribe.
  3. Tap Add. Events appear in the Calendar app within a minute.

On a Mac, the same button opens Calendar. In Google Calendar, use the Google button, or paste https://honestlittleapps.github.io/calendars/menopause-symptom-check-in-calendar/menopause-check-ins.ics into Other calendars › From URL.

Questions

Does the calendar collect my symptoms?

No. It's a read-only reminder. Nothing you log is sent anywhere by subscribing.

Which symptoms should I track?

Common ones are hot flushes and night sweats, sleep, cycle changes, mood, brain fog, joint pain and vaginal or urinary symptoms. Track what's affecting you.

What does My Midlife Story do?

It's a private menopause and perimenopause symptom tracker that turns your logs into a one-page summary for doctor visits. No account needed.

Is this calendar free?

Yes. It costs nothing, needs no account and shows no ads. You can unsubscribe at any time.

How do I remove it from my iPhone?

Open the Calendar app, tap Calendars at the bottom, tap the ⓘ next to this calendar and choose Delete Calendar. Or go to Settings › Apps › Calendar › Calendar Accounts › Subscribed Calendars.
