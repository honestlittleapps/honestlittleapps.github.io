How to Ask for a Second Date

Six ready-to-send messages, when to send them, and how to handle the answer either way.

The best way to ask for a second date is soon, specific and easy to say no to. Soon shows you mean it. Specific (a day and a plan) makes it simple to say yes. Easy to decline keeps it respectful.

Copy-and-send examples

Right after the date, in personI had a really good time tonight. I’d love to do this again. Are you free next week?
By text, the same eveningGot home. Thanks for tonight, I really enjoyed it. Want to try that ramen place next week?
By text, the next dayStill smiling about the mini golf. Would you be up for round two this weekend? I’ll let you pick the place.
If a few days have passedHey! I kept meaning to text. I had a great time on Thursday and would love to see you again. Free on Saturday afternoon?
Suggesting something specificThere’s a free jazz night at the library on Friday. Want to come with me?
Leaving the door openI’d love to see you again if you’re up for it. No pressure either way. Let me know.

What makes it work

  • Name a plan. “That taco place on Friday” beats “we should hang out sometime.”
  • Mention something from the first date. It shows you were listening.
  • Don’t double-text a no-reply for days. One friendly follow-up is enough.
  • Respect a no. Thank them and move on.

Questions

How soon should you ask for a second date?

If you both had a good time, asking at the end of the date or within a day or two is normal and clear. Waiting a week can read as lukewarm.

What if they say no?

Thank them for being honest and leave it there: “Thanks for letting me know, I enjoyed meeting you.” A kind no is still a good outcome.

Is it OK to ask by text?

Yes. Texting is the most common way, and it gives the other person time to answer honestly.
