How Thunderstorms Form (and How to Stay Safe): free lesson plan

A ready-to-teach 40-minute lesson with objectives, a step-by-step plan, a printable worksheet and exit tickets. Free, no sign-up.

Worksheet + exit tickets (PDF)

Objectives

  • Explain in three steps how a thunderstorm forms.
  • Estimate how far away lightning is and follow 'When thunder roars, go indoors.'

Supports weather and climate units: describing weather patterns and hazards, and how people reduce the impact of weather hazards.

Materials

  • A clear cup of warm water and a plate of ice (optional demo)
  • A stopwatch or a clock with a second hand

Lesson steps

  1. Warm-up (5 min). Ask what students hear, see and feel in a thunderstorm. List their answers.
  2. Three steps (10 min). 1) Warm, moist air rises. 2) It cools, and water vapour condenses into a tall cumulonimbus cloud. 3) Ice and water bump around inside the cloud and build up electric charge until it sparks as lightning. Lightning heats the air so fast that it expands with a bang: thunder.
  3. Count the storm (10 min). Sound travels about a mile every 5 seconds. Practise: if you count 10 seconds between the flash and the thunder, the lightning is about 2 miles away. Do three practice problems.
  4. Safety (10 min). Teach the National Weather Service rule: 'When thunder roars, go indoors.' If you can hear thunder, you're close enough to be struck. Wait 30 minutes after the last thunder before going back out.
  5. Exit ticket (5 min). Students write the three steps and solve one distance problem.
