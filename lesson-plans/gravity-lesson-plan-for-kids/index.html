Gravity: Why Do Things Fall? A free lesson plan

A ready-to-teach 40-minute lesson with objectives, a step-by-step plan, a printable worksheet and exit tickets. Free, no sign-up.

Worksheet + exit tickets (PDF)

Objectives

  • Predict and test whether heavy and light objects fall at the same rate.
  • Explain the role of air resistance using the Moon's hammer-and-feather test.

Supports forces and motion units: gravity pulls objects toward Earth's centre; air resistance changes how things fall.

Materials

  • Two balls of different weights (e.g. a tennis ball and a golf ball)
  • Two sheets of paper
  • A chair to stand on (teacher only)

Lesson steps

  1. Predict (5 min). Ask: if I drop a heavy ball and a light ball at the same time, which lands first? Students vote.
  2. Test (10 min). Drop the two balls from the same height several times. They land together. Then drop a flat sheet of paper and a crumpled sheet: the flat one floats down slowly. Ask why.
  3. Explain (10 min). Gravity pulls everything down at the same rate; air pushes back on wide, light things (air resistance). On the Moon there's no air, so in 1971 astronaut David Scott dropped a hammer and a feather on Apollo 15 and they hit the ground together. The Moon's gravity is about one-sixth of Earth's.
  4. Apply (10 min). Students design a paper 'parachute' to fall as slowly as possible and test it.
  5. Exit ticket (5 min). Why did the flat paper fall slower than the crumpled paper?
